## Overview
This document provides a critical analysis of the outcomes written in Amelia's EHCP, with suggested improvements shown in **bold** for additions and ~~strikethrough~~ for deletions.

## **OUTCOME E1: Communication Skills**

### Current Version:
> On a daily basis, Amelia will be able to independently initiate conversations and seek assistance through questions. Amelia will demonstrate increased confidence in her own communication skills through use of a self-rating scale (0-10) representing her feelings of communicative confidence in different contexts.

### Critical Issues:
- **Vague timeframe**: "On a daily basis" - how many times? In what contexts?
- **Lack of baseline**: No starting point mentioned
- **Self-rating scale concerns**: A 0-10 scale for an autistic young person with anxiety may not be reliable or valid
- **Too broad**: Combines multiple skills (initiation, seeking help, confidence) into one outcome
- **Missing specificity**: What types of conversations? With whom?

### Improved Version:
> ~~On a daily basis, Amelia will be able to independently initiate conversations and seek assistance through questions. Amelia will demonstrate increased confidence in her own communication skills through use of a self-rating scale (0-10) representing her feelings of communicative confidence in different contexts.~~
>
> **By [specific review date], Amelia will independently initiate at least 3 conversations per day with familiar adults in low-demand contexts (e.g., at home, with known tutors), as observed and recorded by her LSA and parents.**
>
> **By [specific review date], Amelia will ask for help or clarification at least twice per tutoring session when she encounters difficulties, using her preferred communication method (verbal, break card, or written), as evidenced by tutor logs.**
>
> **By [specific review date], Amelia will use an autism-friendly visual scale (e.g., 5-point emoji scale or zones of regulation) to communicate her comfort level in social situations, with staff recording her selections showing increased willingness to engage in 75% of familiar social contexts compared to baseline data from [date].**

## **OUTCOME E2: Social Participation**

### Current Version:
> Amelia will be able to participate in small group activities with up to three peers who share similar interests and hobbies, for 20 minutes, three times weekly.

### Critical Issues:
- **No timeframe**: When will this be achieved by?
- **Definition unclear**: What does "participate" mean? Just presence, or active engagement?
- **Assumption about peers**: Assumes peer relationships exist - may need building first
- **Rigid specification**: "three times weekly" may be unrealistic given health fluctuations
- **Missing context**: Where? Under what conditions?
- **No progression**: Doesn't account for building up to this level

### Improved Version:
> ~~Amelia will be able to participate in small group activities with up to three peers who share similar interests and hobbies, for 20 minutes, three times weekly.~~
>
> **By [6-month review date], Amelia will actively engage in small group activities (archery club, animal shelter volunteering, or interest-based online groups) with up to 2-3 peers who share her interests, for a minimum of 15 minutes per session, at least twice weekly when her pain and anxiety levels permit, as evidenced by LSA observations and activity logs.**
>
> **"Active engagement" is defined as: participating in the shared activity, responding to at least one peer comment or question per session, and remaining in the space without requiring sensory breaks (though breaks may be taken if needed).**
>
> **By [12-month review date], Amelia will increase session duration to 20-25 minutes and frequency to three times weekly, maintaining active engagement as defined above.**

## **OUTCOME E3: Academic Progress in Literacy and Numeracy**

### Current Version:
> Amelia will be meeting age related expectations in literacy (specifically spelling, punctuation and grammar) and numeracy as evidenced by appropriate assessments and ongoing teacher evaluations.

### Critical Issues:
- **Unrealistic expectation**: Given dyslexia, dysgraphia, ADHD, and significant time out of education, "age-related expectations" may be unachievable and inappropriate
- **No timeframe**: When?
- **Ignores learning profile**: Doesn't account for spiky profile and specific learning difficulties
- **"Appropriate assessments" is vague**: What assessments?
- **Focuses on deficits**: Emphasizes SPaG (spelling, punctuation, grammar) which are her areas of difficulty
- **No baseline or progress measure**: How much progress from where?
- **Potentially damaging**: Sets her up for failure and doesn't celebrate her strengths (science)

### Improved Version:
> ~~Amelia will be meeting age related expectations in literacy (specifically spelling, punctuation and grammar) and numeracy as evidenced by appropriate assessments and ongoing teacher evaluations.~~
>
> **By [annual review date], Amelia will demonstrate at least 12 months' progress in reading comprehension from her baseline of [current level], as measured by [specific assessment tool], enabling her to access age-appropriate science texts independently.**
>
> **By [annual review date], Amelia will demonstrate at least 8-10 months' progress in functional literacy skills (spelling, punctuation, grammar) from baseline [current level], as measured by standardized assessments, while consistently using at least two assistive technologies (e.g., speech-to-text, word processor with grammar support) to compensate for areas of difficulty and produce work that reflects her verbal ability.**
>
> **By [annual review date], Amelia will demonstrate at least 12 months' progress in numeracy from baseline [current level], as measured by [specific assessment], achieving the mathematical skills required for GCSE Science courses and daily living tasks.**
>
> **Progress will be monitored through half-termly standardized assessments and ongoing teacher observations, with accommodations made for health-related absences and high pain days.**

## **OUTCOME E4: Alternative Recording Methods**

### Current Version:
> Amelia will utilise at least two alternative methods for recording information in academic settings, such as speech-to-text software or word processors and report a positive impact on her learning experience.

### Critical Issues:
- **No timeframe**: When?
- **Too low-level**: This is more of a provision/strategy than an outcome
- **"Report positive impact" is weak**: How measured? Subjective and vague
- **Focuses on tools rather than achievement**: What will she achieve using these tools?
- **Passive language**: "will utilise" - not ambitious enough

### Improved Version:
> ~~Amelia will utilise at least two alternative methods for recording information in academic settings, such as speech-to-text software or word processors and report a positive impact on her learning experience.~~
>
> **By [3-month review date], Amelia will be independently using at least two assistive recording methods (e.g., Dragon speech-to-text, word processor with predictive text, graphic organizers, or graphics tablet) across all subject areas, as evidenced by tutor logs and work samples.**
>
> **By [6-month review date], Amelia will produce written work using assistive technology that demonstrates her knowledge and understanding at a level commensurate with her verbal ability, submitting at least 80% of assessed work in this format, as evidenced by completed assignments and teacher feedback.**
>
> **By [annual review date], Amelia will self-report (using agreed communication method) that using assistive technology reduces her anxiety about written tasks and allows her to express her ideas more fully, as documented in half-termly review meetings with her EOTAS coordinator.**

## **OUTCOME E5: Emotional Expression**

### Current Version:
> Amelia will demonstrate the ability to express her emotions using an agreed strategy (verbally or non-verbally) to trusted adults in 90% of situations where she experiences heightened emotional states.

### Critical Issues:
- **90% is arbitrary**: Where does this percentage come from? Is it evidence-based?
- **No timeframe**: When?
- **"Agreed strategy" undefined**: What strategy? Agreed with whom?
- **"Heightened emotional states" vague**: What counts as heightened?
- **How is this measured?**: Who counts the situations and determines the percentage?
- **Doesn't account for shutdowns**: During shutdowns, she cannot communicate - this outcome penalizes her for autism symptoms
- **Missing progression**: Assumes ability exists, just needs expression

### Improved Version:
> ~~Amelia will demonstrate the ability to express her emotions using an agreed strategy (verbally or non-verbally) to trusted adults in 90% of situations where she experiences heightened emotional states.~~
>
> **By [3-month review date], Amelia and her support team will co-create a personalised emotional communication toolkit including at least 5 methods for expressing emotions (e.g., Zones of Regulation chart, emotion cards, break card, written notes, or verbal expression when able), documented in her support plan.**
>
> **By [6-month review date], Amelia will use at least one method from her emotional communication toolkit to indicate her emotional state to a trusted adult before reaching crisis point (shutdown/meltdown) in 6 out of 10 instances of rising distress, as recorded in daily wellbeing logs.**
>
> **By [annual review date], Amelia will successfully communicate her emotional needs using her toolkit in 7-8 out of 10 instances of emotional dysregulation, allowing adults to provide appropriate support and reducing the frequency of shutdowns and meltdowns by [specific percentage based on baseline data].**
>
> **Note: During shutdowns, Amelia will not be expected to communicate and will be provided with a quiet, safe space to regulate, as per her sensory support plan. Recovery time will not be counted as a failure to meet this outcome.**

## **OUTCOME E7: Sensory Regulation Strategies**

### Current Version:
> Amelia will identify and utilise at least three effective strategies when sensory overload occurs, for example making use of a quiet space in the learning environment, as verified by education leaders.

### Critical Issues:
- **No timeframe**: When?
- **Reactive rather than proactive**: Focuses on response to overload, not prevention
- **"Identify and utilise" - are these the same skill?**: Should be separated
- **"Verified by education leaders" is problematic**: During overload, she may shut down and be unable to communicate - adults observing may not know what strategy she's using
- **"At least three" - why three?**: Quality over quantity
- **Doesn't account for inability to implement during severe overload**: May be impossible during meltdown/shutdown
- **Examples given are provision, not outcome**: Having quiet space is provision, not outcome

### Improved Version:
> ~~Amelia will identify and utilise at least three effective strategies when sensory overload occurs, for example making use of a quiet space in the learning environment, as verified by education leaders.~~
>
> **By [3-month review date], Amelia will work with her occupational therapist to identify at least 5 sensory regulation strategies that work for her specific sensory profile (e.g., using quiet space, noise-cancelling headphones, weighted blanket, movement breaks, proprioceptive activities), documented in her sensory support plan.**
>
> **By [6-month review date], Amelia will recognise early warning signs of sensory overload (e.g., increased irritability, difficulty concentrating, physical discomfort) and independently implement at least one proactive regulation strategy before reaching crisis point, in 5 out of 10 instances, as recorded in daily sensory logs by LSA and parents.**
>
> **By [annual review date], Amelia will independently implement appropriate sensory regulation strategies when she notices early signs of dysregulation, in 7 out of 10 instances, reducing the frequency of sensory meltdowns/shutdowns by [specific percentage based on baseline data], as evidenced by sensory logs and incident records.**
>
> **By [annual review date], Amelia will communicate to at least one trusted adult which sensory strategies are most effective for her in different situations (e.g., "noise-cancelling headphones work best at archery," "quiet space works best during tutoring"), demonstrating increased self-awareness and ability to advocate for her sensory needs.**
>
> **Note: During severe sensory overload resulting in shutdown, Amelia will be provided with immediate environmental modifications and safe space without expectation of strategy implementation, as this may not be possible during crisis.**

## **OVERALL CRITICAL OBSERVATIONS:**

### **Major Issues Across All Outcomes:**

1. **No timeframes**: None of the original outcomes specify when they should be achieved by
2. **Not SMART**: Most lack Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound criteria
3. **No baselines**: No starting points mentioned, making progress impossible to measure
4. **Arbitrary percentages**: 80%, 90% appear without evidence or rationale
5. **Ableist expectations**: E3 expects age-related expectations despite significant learning difficulties
6. **Provision disguised as outcomes**: E4 focuses on using tools rather than what she'll achieve with them
7. **Combined outcomes**: E6 inappropriately combines two unrelated skills
8. **Lack of progression**: No stepped approach or acknowledgment of building up skills
9. **Ignore health fluctuations**: CRPS flares and pain not adequately considered in outcomes
10. **Not child-centered**: Written in professional language, not accessible to Amelia
11. **Verification issues**: Who measures? How? When?
12. **No parent/child input visible**: Outcomes don't clearly reflect what Amelia wants to achieve
13. **Focus on deficits not strengths**: E3 emphasizes literacy weaknesses rather than building on science strengths

### **Recommended Actions:**

1. ✅ **Add specific review dates** to all outcomes (e.g., "By February 2026...")
2. ✅ **Establish baselines** before setting targets (current levels must be recorded)
3. ✅ **Break down complex outcomes** into smaller, stepped goals
4. ✅ **Make outcomes measurable** with clear criteria and evidence methods
5. ✅ **Ensure outcomes reflect aspirations** from Section A
6. ✅ **Account for health conditions** in targets (CRPS, anxiety, autism)
7. ✅ **Use person-centered language** that Amelia can understand
8. ✅ **Focus on achievements** not just service delivery
9. ✅ **Separate provision from outcomes** clearly
10. ✅ **Include Amelia and parents** in outcome-setting process

### **Strengths of Current Outcomes:**

- E2 includes specific, measurable details (number of peers, duration, frequency)
- E4 acknowledges need for alternative methods (though implementation as outcome is weak)
- E5 includes both verbal and non-verbal options
- E7 provides an example to illustrate what is meant
- Outcomes generally address important areas for Amelia's development
