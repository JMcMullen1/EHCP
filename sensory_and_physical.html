NEED SENSORY 1
Amelia presents with sensory processing and regulation difficulties impacting on her ability to attend and concentrate for learning, emotional, regulation and her independence skills and social interaction.
Outcomes
E7: Amelia will identify and utilise at least three effective strategies when sensory overload occurs, for example making use of a quiet space in the learning environment, as verified by education leaders.

SMART Revision: By [end of term/specific date], Amelia will demonstrate effective sensory regulation through: (1) Proactively using at least [3] preventative sensory regulation strategies (e.g., scheduled sensory breaks every [X] minutes, use of noise-cancelling headphones, fidget tools) independently in 70% of learning sessions, increasing from baseline of [X%]; (2) When sensory overload occurs, independently recognizing early warning signs and implementing response strategies (quiet space use, sensory toolkit, break requests) within [2 minutes] of onset in 60% of episodes, improving from baseline of [X%]; (3) Frequency of sensory overload episodes decreasing from average of [X per day/week] to [Y per day/week]; (4) Sustained attention during learning tasks improving from average of [X minutes] to [Y minutes] as measured by time-sampling observations. Progress tracked via daily sensory logs, observation records, and attention duration data collected [weekly/daily].

Note: This outcome is weak as: (1) It only addresses response to sensory overload after it occurs, not prevention or ongoing sensory regulation; (2) "Identify and utilise" lacks specificity about independence level, frequency, or effectiveness; (3) "Verified by education leaders" is vague about measurement method; (4) It does not address the broader impact on attention, concentration, emotional regulation, and social interaction mentioned in the need. This should be redesigned to include: proactive sensory regulation strategies, measurable improvements in attention/concentration/emotional regulation, baseline and target data for strategy use, and objective measures of reduced sensory overload frequency/intensity.

NEED MOTOR 3
CRPS causes episodes of pain that can vary in duration; some may last only a few weeks, while others may persist for a more extended period. Amelia's pain flares can be so debilitating that she is unable to get out of bed. During severe periods, Amelia is wheelchair-bound and requires physical support and environmental adaptations to manage her daily activities.
Outcomes
E6: Amelia will successfully transition to a full time EOTIS package, maintaining active engagement in her educational activities at least 80% of the time, whether attending in-person or through alternative educational provisions. She will be able to request support with personal care and mobility from a key adult when required.

SMART Revision: By [end of academic year/specific date], Amelia will access education through a flexible EOTAS package that accommodates CRPS pain variability: (1) During low-moderate pain periods (Level 1-3 on agreed pain scale), maintain 70% engagement in educational activities for [X] hours per week, increasing from baseline of [Y] hours; (2) During severe pain episodes (Level 4-5), access alternative educational delivery (online, recorded lessons, modified activities) with engagement target of 40% to maintain curriculum access; (3) Environmental adaptations for wheelchair accessibility will be in place and audited [termly], with 100% of learning spaces meeting access requirements; (4) Independently request support for personal care and mobility from key adults in 80% of situations when needed, using agreed communication methods (verbal or alternative); (5) Implement and use documented pain management strategies [X] times per day during educational activities; (6) Functional mobility goals: achieve [specific SMART mobility goal related to physiotherapy targets] as measured by [physiotherapy assessment]. Success measured through attendance logs, engagement tracking by pain level, accessibility audits, support request logs, and physiotherapy progress reports.

Note: This outcome is weak and does not adequately address this need for several reasons: (1) "Successfully transition to full time EOTAS" is about placement/attendance rather than managing CRPS pain episodes and mobility needs; (2) The 80% engagement target does not account for the variability and severity of CRPS pain flares described in the need; (3) "Request support with personal care and mobility" is minimal compared to the need's description of being wheelchair-bound and unable to get out of bed during severe periods; (4) There are no measures related to pain management, environmental adaptations, or functional mobility during different phases of CRPS. This should be redesigned to include: specific supports during pain flares, environmental adaptations for wheelchair access, pain management strategies with measurable outcomes, functional mobility goals across different pain levels, and realistic expectations for engagement that account for severe pain episodes.
